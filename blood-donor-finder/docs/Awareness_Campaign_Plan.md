## Overview

The LifeLine Awareness Campaign is structured in three phases, informed directly by community survey findings. Each phase targets a distinct goal: building visibility, facilitating actual donation, and creating a self-sustaining outreach model.

---

## Phase 1 — Awareness Drive (Weeks 1–3)

**Goal:** Make the community aware that blood shortage is a local issue and that LifeLine exists.

### Channels

| Channel | Format | Target Barrier Addressed |
|---------|--------|--------------------------|
| College notice boards & email | A3 poster + QR code to LifeLine | Lack of Awareness |
| WhatsApp broadcast lists | 3-card infographic (myth-busting) | Fear of Needles |
| Instagram / Facebook Reels | 30-second "one donation, three lives" reel | Lack of Awareness |
| Faculty announcement slots | 5-min talk with slide deck | Lack of Awareness |
| Housing society notice boards | Bilingual (English + Marathi) leaflet | No Nearby Camp |

### Key Messages (mapped to survey barriers)

- **"It takes 10 minutes. It saves three lives."** (addresses Lack of Time)
- **"Donating blood is safer than you think."** *(Fear of Needles)*
  - Include infographic showing the needle used is fine-bore and process is supervised by medical staff.
- **"Know your blood group. It could save your life one day."** *(Awareness gap)*
- **"A camp is coming to your locality."** *(No Nearby Camp)*

### Deliverables

- [ ] 500 printed leaflets (A5, bilingual)
- [ ] 3 Instagram Reels + 1 Facebook post
- [ ] LifeLine URL and QR code on all materials
- [ ] Week-3 reach report (form impressions + website visits)

## Phase 2 — Donation Camp (Week 4–6)

**Goal:** Convert awareness into action by holding a supervised blood donation camp.

### Camp Details (planned)

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Venue | College auditorium or community hall (accessible, air-conditioned) |
| Partner | Janwadi Blood Bank / Red Cross Chapter |
| Medical supervision | 1 MBBS doctor + 2 trained technicians (provided by blood bank) |
| Target donors | 80 first-time donors |
| Eligibility check | On-site by blood bank staff (NOT by LifeLine) |
| Refreshments | Juice + biscuits provided post-donation |
| Slots | 9 AM–1 PM and 3 PM–6 PM (flexible timing per survey feedback) |

### Addressing Barriers at the Camp

| Barrier | Mitigation |
|---------|------------|
| Fear of Needles | Calm environment; buddy system (donate with a friend); explain steps beforehand |
| Health Concerns | On-site doctor answers questions; clear exclusion criteria displayed |
| Lack of Time | Pre-registration via LifeLine to allocate time slots; <45 min total process |
| No Nearby Camp | Venue in a central, accessible location with two-wheeler parking |

### Pre-Camp Registration

Donors are invited to register on LifeLine (marking themselves available) and note the camp date from the Campaigns page. Actual slot allocation is managed by the blood bank partner.

## Phase 3 — Sustained Outreach (Months 2–6)

**Goal:** Maintain momentum and build a recurring donation culture.

### Activities

1. **Monthly social media posts** — real donor stories (anonymised), blood-need alerts, camp announcements.
2. **Feedback loop** — analyse LifeLine feedback ratings and survey responses monthly; adapt messaging.
3. **Inter-college competition** — track which college contributes the most registered donors (with opt-in consent); top college gets a certificate.
4. **Hospital partnership** — Collaborate with 2–3 local hospitals to display LifeLine QR codes in OPD waiting areas.
5. **Quarterly camps** — Aim for 1 camp every 3 months.
6. **Dashboard transparency** — Make aggregated stats (total donors, requests fulfilled) public on the LifeLine homepage as social proof.

---

## Messaging Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|------|---------|
| Emphasise voluntary, informed consent | Create guilt or pressure |
| Credit blood banks and healthcare staff for the clinical work | Suggest LifeLine performs any medical function |
| Share aggregate impact numbers | Share individual donor details |
| Acknowledge all valid reasons for not donating | Dismiss health concerns |
| Use inclusive language (all genders, ages 18–65) | Target only young men |
